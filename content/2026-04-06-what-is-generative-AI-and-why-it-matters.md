Every wave of software eventually gets a moment where the underlying shift is bigger than the hype around it. Generative AI is having that moment now. Not because it's new — the ideas behind it go back decades — but because it just crossed a threshold where the output is good enough to actually use, not just marvel at.

## What "Generative" Actually Means

**Generative model** — A model trained to produce new content resembling its training data, rather than just labeling or sorting existing data. A spam filter is discriminative — it sorts. A model that writes an email from scratch is generative — it creates something that didn't exist before.

**Beyond text** — Generative AI isn't limited to language. The same underlying idea powers image generation, code generation, audio synthesis, and video — anything where a model learns the patterns of a domain well enough to produce plausible new examples of it.

## Why Now, Specifically

- Scale: models trained on far more data and compute than a few years ago capture far more nuanced patterns.
- Accessibility: what used to require a research lab now runs through an API call or a chat interface.
- Instruction-following: models shifted from "complete this text" to "do what I asked," which is what made them usable by non-experts.

## Where It's Actually Being Used

Generative AI shows up anywhere a first draft, a summary, or a starting point saves real time — drafting emails, summarizing documents, generating code scaffolding, producing marketing copy, or answering support questions. The common thread isn't replacing judgment, it's removing the blank-page problem.

> Generative AI doesn't replace expertise. It replaces the empty page you used to stare at before applying that expertise.

## What It Still Gets Wrong

These models generate plausible text, not verified truth — a fluent, confident-sounding answer can still be factually wrong. They don't know what they don't know unless explicitly told, and they reflect patterns in their training data, biases included. Treating output as a draft to review, not a finished answer to trust, is still the right instinct.

## Why This Matters Beyond the Hype

The practical shift is that a huge category of "I need a first version of this" tasks got dramatically cheaper. That's not artificial general intelligence — it's a genuinely useful tool with real limits, and understanding where those limits sit is what separates people who use it well from people who get burned by it.
